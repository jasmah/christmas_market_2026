### Currency & Cash Strategy for 4 Adults

Even though contactless payments (Apple Pay, Google Pay, and tap-to-pay credit cards) have expanded across Europe, **physical cash remains essential at German and Austrian Christmas markets**, whereas **Prague is predominantly contactless**.

* **Munich & Nuremberg (Germany) — Euro (€ / EUR):**
* **Why You Need Cash:** Roughly 40% to 50% of food and craft stalls in Bavaria are still cash-only, and almost all hot drink stalls require cash for the **mug deposit (*Pfand*)**. Every mug of *Glühwein* or *Kinderpunsch* costs roughly €4.50–€6.00 plus a **€3.00–€5.00 refundable deposit per mug**. For 4 adults ordering a single round of drinks, you will hand over €35–€45 upfront and receive €12–€20 back in cash when you return the mugs and deposit tokens (*Pfandmarken*).
* Recommended Cash for 4 Adults in Munich (Dec 20–22): **€200 – €250 EUR** total in small bills (€5, €10, and €20 notes) to cover 2.5 days of snacks, *Pfand* floats, and cash-only ornaments.


* Recommended Cash for 4 Adults in Nuremberg (Dec 21 Day Trip): **€120 – €150 EUR** total to cover rounds of grilled sausages, ginger-spice *Lebkuchen* tins, drink deposits, and traditional handmade prune-figure souvenirs (*Zwetschgenmännla*).




* **Prague (Czechia) — Czech Koruna (Kč / CZK):**
* **Why Cards Win Here:** Czechia uses the Czech Koruna (CZK), not the Euro. Roughly 85% to 90% of Prague Christmas market huts accept tap-to-pay cards, and drinks are served in paper cups (no ceramic mug deposit required). **Never pay with Euro cash at Prague market stalls**—vendors who accept Euros apply punitive exchange rates (often 15%–20% worse than bank rates). Always tap your card in CZK (decline "Currency Conversion / DCC" on the terminal) or use local CZK notes.
* Recommended Cash for 4 Adults in Prague (Dec 23–25): **1,500 – 2,500 CZK** total (approx. **$90 – $150 CAD** / €60 – €100 EUR) for the occasional cash-only craft stall, roasted chestnut cart, or 10–20 CZK coin-operated public restrooms.




* **Vienna (Austria) — Euro (€ / EUR):**
* **Why You Need Cash:** About 60% of Vienna's stalls now take cards, but several food huts, chestnut roasters (*Maroni*), and the €4.00–€5.00 ceramic mug deposits (*Häferlpfand*) still run much faster with cash.
* Recommended Cash for 4 Adults in Vienna (Dec 26–27): **€150 – €200 EUR** total for the group.




* **Total Cash Summary for the 4-Market Loop:**
* **Euros (Munich + Nuremberg + Vienna):** **€470 – €600 EUR total** for 4 adults.
* **Czech Koruna (Prague):** **1,500 – 2,500 CZK total** for 4 adults.
* (Note for Dec 28–31 in Glasgow & Edinburgh: Scotland uses the **British Pound (£ / GBP)** and is nearly 100% cashless; £30–£40 GBP in pocket cash for tips or small purchases is plenty).


* **ATM Pro Tip:** Avoid standalone blue-and-yellow *Euronet* ATMs in all four cities (they charge steep markups and fees). Use ATMs physically attached to major banks (*Sparkasse* or *Deutsche Bank* in Germany; *Česká spořitelna*, *ČSOB*, or *Komerční banka* in Prague; *Erste Bank* or *Bank Austria* in Vienna).

### General Christmas Market Insider Tips & Tricks for Your Group of 4

* **The "Table Scout" System for 4 Adults:** Standing high-top tables (*Stehtische*) around food and Glühwein stalls fill up fast between 5:00 PM and 8:00 PM. Split into two pairs: have two people claim a spot at a *Stehtisch* (politely asking *"Ist hier noch frei?"* — "Is there room here?" — as sharing standing tables with locals is standard etiquette) while the other two buy the four drinks and sausages.
* **Collecting vs. Returning Mugs:** Each market (and sometimes individual stalls within the same city) has its own custom year-dated ceramic boot or mug. If you want to keep a mug as a souvenir, simply walk away with it (your €3–€5 *Pfand* deposit covers the purchase)—better yet, ask the vendor *"Können wir eine frische Tasse zum Mitnehmen haben?"* ("Can we trade this used mug for a clean/unwashed one to take home?"), and they will gladly hand you a clean one from behind the counter!
* **Footwear Beats Coat Thickness:** Standing on frozen cobblestones for 4–6 hours drains body heat straight through thin rubber sneaker soles. Wear waterproof boots coupled with thick merino wool socks (or slip toe-warmers into your shoes), and dress in breathable layers you can unzip when stepping into heated U-Bahn cars or museum cafes.
* **Pickpocket & Bag Awareness:** Crowds pack shoulder-to-shoulder around the Marienplatz glockenspiel, Nuremberg's *Schöner Brunnen*, and Prague's Astronomical Clock at the top of every hour. Keep wallets and phones in zipped front pockets or cross-body bags worn in front.
